## Implemented behavior

A recent-MFA exact-grant staff statement operation archives the committed prior ledger revision and an immutable statement-mail intent before both commit atomically with one pending delivery row. Intent identity is UUIDv5 of the statement; exact statement retries recover/verify that intent. Historical statements are not silently backfilled: an authorized exact statement retry can enqueue its captured revision. The sole recipient is the fixed `support-statement@synthetic.invalid` fixture. Body contains owner-safe task work, cumulative/reserved/remaining integer seconds and the committed revision; no real email address, staff actor/MFA data, bearer or technical private input is collected.

The existing `run_worker`/`services.execute_once` lifecycle processes configured local statement jobs before disposable probes. No new daemon/queue technology. Configure the existing private local archive through `SHOP_LOCAL_CHECKOUT_ARCHIVE_ROOT`; an absent root leaves statements pending. Worker reads only an already owned archive, never initializes/adopts one. Short PostgreSQL `SKIP LOCKED` claim transactions establish finite1–60-second leases, stable effect identity, fresh tokens and1–5 attempts (default3). Exhausted expired claims become held; no unbounded retry/reopen interface.

After claim commit the local fake sink locks/rechecks the original staff credential/generation, active staff user, current verified active customer and exact support assignment, then grant and claim token. Original archive/acceptance/captured-payment and complete ledger history through the exact statement revision are verified before capture. Async processing relies on the archived recent-MFA intent and current credential/scope; it does not reuse a browser MFA session or demand that its original five-minute freshness window remain open. Credential reset/revoke or assignment removal blocks pending capture; no automatic reauthorization.

An immutable one-per-intent capture row and its archived/read-back receipt commit independently of delivery acknowledgement. It is a durable local database/archive sink, not the old in-memory `CaptureMail` and not an SMTP delivery guarantee. A response lost after sink commit can reclaim the expired lease, verify/reuse the same capture and acknowledge it without another work/ledger command. Stale/expired tokens cannot acknowledge or start a new capture. Prepared orphan bytes alone do not complete delivery. Pending/claimed/captured/held status is exposed only in existing resource-authorized private support summaries; captured status requires recoverable intent/capture archives. No email-sent claim.

SQL triggers prohibit intent/capture update/delete. Mutable delivery rows have bounded attempts and consistent state/token/lease constraints. Mail failures never create another debit; they also cannot retroactively undo already committed work. Statements created after corrections retain the committed reverse/replace history. Later work does not alter an earlier statement body.
