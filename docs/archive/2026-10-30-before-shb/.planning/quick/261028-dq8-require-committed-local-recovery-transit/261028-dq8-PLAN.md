<objective>
Require the existing local recovery transition to commit before reporting successful quarantine or release. Reject an enclosing transaction or disabled autocommit before invalidating any access state.
</objective>

<design_critique>
Current local_recovery_operation uses an ordinary nested atomic block. A caller can receive success while another PostgreSQL connection still sees the prior access state, then roll back the transition. Reuse the established effect-admission autocommit requirement and a durable atomic block. The internal fixture interface, exact database identity, immutable retry identities, selected staff reset and release/drain checks remain. Ordinary top-level failures still roll back together. No new recovery evidence format, provider contract, dependency or external operation is required. Execute inline under the current Codex skill adapter; no agent dispatch or worktree is needed.
</design_critique>

<threat_model>
- A successful-looking but uncommitted quarantine must not be treated as an effective access fence.
- An uncommitted release must not produce a completion result that its caller can later roll back.
- Denied calls, including selected-restore cleanup, must leave sessions, challenges, credentials, grants, business records, epochs and history unchanged.
- Successful top-level transitions and exact retries must be visible to an independent connection before return, with no weakening of unfinished-effect or current-hold checks.
- Local synthetic evidence cannot establish actual isolation, post-point reconciliation, custody, MFA recovery or full acceptance.
</threat_model>
