<objective>
Reproduce and fence stale customer checkout acceptance, payment and fulfilment commands across local recovery, preserving one-way bank uncertainty and internal lookup.
</objective>

<design_critique>
First reproduce normal customer HTTP requests whose parser completes quarantine or quarantine/release before service entry. The current services receive only the cached owner. If confirmed, pass the originating customer request to existing transaction boundaries and lock its valid epoch before owner/resource work. Reuse one shared checkout authority predicate from the support path. Acceptance has two durable transactions separated by archive work; both must recheck. Fulfilment has archived preparation before grant/stage commit; recheck at that commit. Payment needs origin binding at dispatch admission as well as its domain transactions, because quarantine/release can occur after binding the prepared attempt. Extend the existing local-effect admission with an optional expected epoch for that one call, atomically compare it under the existing epoch lock and preserve default internal callers. Never hold a database lock across bank/lookup I/O, add a retryable claim, or prevent internal read-only reconciliation while held. Execute inline.
</design_critique>

<threat_model>
- A stale customer request must not accept, start a new fake bank operation, create support/licence grants or complete a delivery stage after quarantine/release.
- Quarantine during archive work must prevent final acceptance/grant/stage commit; prepared evidence never constitutes completion.
- A prepared payment bound before reset must not adopt a new released epoch at dispatch. A reset after its one-way claim leaves uncertainty and requires lookup, never another start.
- Lock the epoch before owner/resource work and observe it from a separate real PostgreSQL connection. No bank/lookup I/O may run in a caller transaction.
- Preserve immutable snapshots, exact retries, deadlines, staff scope, staged obligations and synthetic-only gates. No actual approval, money, licence, provider or recovery qualification.
</threat_model>
